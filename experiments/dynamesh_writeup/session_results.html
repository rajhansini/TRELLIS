What we learned about geometry, texture, and the coords pin

1 · Where an adapter can be placed at all

settled

out_layer is the only contamination-free position

v1 — LoRA inside attention · v3 — LoRA on block MLPs · v4 — LoRA on out_layer

Everything before out_layer mixes information across voxels: windowed self-attention in the 12 transformer blocks, and convolution in the two upsamplers. An adapter placed upstream propagates an edit intended for one region into others. out_layer is a per-voxel linear map with nothing downstream that mixes.

2 · The white cast

confirmed — prediction stated in advance

It was the loss region, not the adapter

TRELLIS's teapot and the video's teapot are different shapes — silhouette IoU 0.76. 16% of the rendered teapot sits on white GT background, so the loss instructs those pixels to become white. That rim is 16% of the pixels but supplies 56% of the total colour gradient, sign [+,+,+] on every channel. The adapter reads voxel features, not position, so it cannot obey that only at the rim — it learns "be whiter" globally.

Predicted, then measured. Modelling the adapter as a global additive shift, least squares over each candidate region predicts a different outcome. Against v4's actual output across all 150 frames: union error 0.027±0.014, intersection error 0.111±0.019 — union closer on 149/150 frames, paired t = 40.0. v4 did exactly what the union region demanded.

3 · Does the texture paste onto unseen surfaces?

no — hypothesis refuted

The edit is uniform at every angle

First multi-view render of any adapter in this project — every previous turntable was the 12-block decoder LoRA, not v4. Two findings, one of them unwelcome.

  • v4's edit is applied uniformly at all angles. It is not a decal correct from the front and wrong elsewhere, so the suspected pasting failure is not present.
  • The frozen baseline already produces correctly wrapped lava all the way around, and v4 degrades it from every viewpoint. Before rung8, the adapter was worse than doing nothing.

4 · Geometry — the main question of the session

pinned coords

The mesh is effectively rigid

Geometry channels [0:53] are decoded from the per-frame SLaT, so in principle the frozen decoder already outputs a mesh that changes shape every frame. Measured directly, it does not.

tracking ratio0.063chamfer signal is 365× the numerical noise floor — the movement is real, just tiny

The sharper statement is the third panel: chamfer rises to 0.005 by frame 20 then goes completely flat. The mesh makes one small adjustment early and freezes for the remaining 130 frames, while the GT deforms monotonically throughout.

free coords — the hypothesis that failed

Unpinning does not recover shape tracking

The 0.063 was measured with coords pinned to frame 75. The obvious worry was that the pin had frozen geometry TRELLIS would otherwise have changed on its own — that the constraint, not the model, was the limitation. So the same measurement was rerun with the sparse structure sampled per frame, seed held constant so only the image varied.

The 1.153 is an artefact, and the plot shows why. It compares the magnitude of TRELLIS's step noise against the magnitude of GT's smooth growth; they happen to be similar in size. But the silhouette is piecewise flat with hard jumps at frames 11 and 33 — and the same two frames produce discontinuities in silhouette area, multi-view IoU and vertex count simultaneously. That is the occupancy grid snapping between discrete configurations, not deformation. The direction is wrong too: TRELLIS ends at −1.95% while GT is at +5.43%.

FlexiCubes documents exactly this: "when the isosurface slips over a grid vertex, the mesh jumps discontinuously." Occupancy is binary, so per-frame sampling snaps between configurations instead of deforming smoothly.

5 · Is the flicker caused by the random seed?

no — it is the conditioning

Pinning the seed helps by 30%, and leaves flicker at 33×

Stock TRELLIS, 150 frames, two arms differing by a single argument: seed=6 on every frame versus seed=frame index. Everything else identical.

Fixing the seed cuts flicker from 47 to 33 — real, but the frames still jump 33× more than the trend requires. The instability is driven by the per-frame image conditioning, not the random draw. Pinning the seed cannot remove it; only pinning coords can.

6 · Where this leaves the geometry plan

open

Neither pinned nor free tracks the video

  • Pinned → mesh frozen, 0.3% movement. Too rigid.
  • Free → jumps between 2–3 discrete states, wrong direction, 20× flicker.

The pin was not the whole problem. The sparse structure is quantised — a voxel is in or out — so it cannot express a smooth 5% deformation regardless of how it is sampled.

That strengthens the case for a geometry adapter rather than weakening it: sdf [0:8] and deform [8:32] at out_layer are continuous, sub-voxel quantities. They can express smooth deformation the discrete occupancy grid structurally cannot.

Supporting evidence, CPU only. The SLaT latent drifts 1.46σ from frame 1 to 150, and temporal variation is 52% of spatial variation. The shape signal does reach the decoder — the decoder routes it into appearance and not geometry. A geometry adapter would be rerouting an existing signal, not inventing one.

7 · What the literature says before we build it

Supported. FlexiCubes is designed for gradient-based mesh optimisation, and the SDF and the α/β/γ dual weights are optimised jointly via autodiff — "these parameters are updated along with the underlying scalar field." Two regularizers are used on every example in that paper: L_dev (penalises deviation between each dual vertex and its edge crossings) and L_sign (penalises sign changes on grid edges, discouraging spurious geometry).

Not supported. Every success found is multi-view — FlexiCubes' own deforming-object experiment is multi-view physics recovery. The single-view literature states the failure mode plainly: "silhouette data do not reflect changes in mesh vertices in the depth direction, which prevents the renderer from propagating gradients to coordinates in the depth direction," producing results "similar to a visual hull lacking detail." The standard remedy is normal or depth supervision.

The angle nobody appears to have tried. Half of the 48 appearance channels at [53:101] are shading normals, not albedo — 8 corners × (3 colour + 3 normal). Normals are precisely what the literature says fixes silhouette-only supervision. That signal is already in the channel block this project has been writing to all along.

9 · Open

  • Geometry adapter is untested. No run has ever written [0:53]. The cheap first check is a single forward+backward measuring whether gradient reaches the geometry rows at all — if it is near zero, the plan is dead before any training.
  • Albedo-only has never been tested. Every run writes all 48 appearance channels — 24 albedo and 24 shading normals. "Colour-only" has never meant colour only.
  • rung8 has no turntable. The white-cast fix is verified head-on; whether it holds off-view is unknown.
  • No adapter is time-conditioned. Temporal variation enters only through the per-frame SLaT. There is no α input anywhere in the code.
