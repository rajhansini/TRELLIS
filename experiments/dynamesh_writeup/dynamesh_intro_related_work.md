# Introduction & Related Work

*Dynamic Texture and Geometry Generation on 3D Meshes via Structured Latent Modulation*

## 1 Background: Progress in 3D Generation

The past several years have witnessed rapid advances in 3D content generation, driven by the confluence of neural implicit representations, large-scale diffusion-based priors, and differentiable rendering. Early work on Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)[1] demonstrated that volumetric scene representations could be reconstructed from 2D images via differentiable volume rendering, enabling high-quality novel view synthesis but at significant inference cost and with limited generalizability beyond a single scene. 3D Gaussian Splatting[2] addressed the speed limitation by representing scenes as learnable Gaussian primitives amenable to real-time rasterization, while methods such as DreamFusion[3] and Magic3D[4] extended generative 3D creation to open-vocabulary text prompts by distilling score gradients from pretrained 2D diffusion models. Despite their generative scope, these approaches produce implicit or Gaussian-based representations that are difficult to integrate into standard graphics pipelines, which demand explicit, textured triangle meshes with well-defined surface topology.

The demand for production-ready 3D assets has motivated a parallel line of research on explicit mesh generation from single images. Feed-forward reconstruction models, including LRM[5], InstantMesh[6], and CraftsMan[7], encode single-view or multi-view observations into latent representations decoded directly to textured meshes, achieving inference in seconds. However, these models tend to produce smooth, under-detailed geometry and coarse textures — reflecting the fundamental difficulty of recovering fine surface structure from sparse 2D observations — and operate in a purely reconstructive, single-shot setting with no mechanism for downstream temporal variation.

## 2 TRELLIS: Structured Latents for High-Fidelity Mesh Generation

A significant advance in high-fidelity mesh generation comes from TRELLIS[8], which introduces *Structured Latent representations (SLaTs)* as a unified medium for image-conditioned 3D generation. TRELLIS operates in two sequential stages. In the first stage, a flow-matching model maps an input image — encoded into semantic tokens via a pretrained DINOv2[9] feature extractor — into a SLaT: a sparse set of approximately 7,300 occupied voxels arranged in a structured 3D grid, each carrying a 768-dimensional latent code that encodes both the structural occupancy and the per-location information required for subsequent decoding. This sparse representation establishes where the object exists in space and what features are needed at each location, with the sparse support determined and fixed at this stage.

In the second stage, a separate flow model refines the per-voxel codes into a full appearance representation, conditioned on the same image features. The resulting SLaT is decoded by the *SLaT Mesh Decoder* — a transformer-based architecture comprising 12 SparseTransformerBlocks that process all voxel features jointly, followed by two SparseSubdivideBlock3d upsamplers that expand the 7,300 coarse voxels to approximately 58,000 fine-grained voxels. A final linear projection layer maps each fine voxel's 96-dimensional features to 101 output channels: 53 encoding geometry (signed distance values, vertex deformations, and FlexiCubes dual-weights) and 48 encoding per-vertex color. The FlexiCubes[10] differentiable mesh extractor then converts these features into an explicit, fully textured triangle mesh. TRELLIS achieves state-of-the-art fidelity in both geometry and texture among feed-forward image-to-3D methods, and its structured latent representation provides a natural substrate for downstream editing and control.

> **The static limitation.** Despite these advances, TRELLIS generates a single, fixed 3D object from a single image — a mesh with frozen geometry and a frozen texture. There is no mechanism for the generated object to express temporal variation: to age, deform, decay, or change surface appearance over time. **DynaMesh** addresses this gap directly, introducing a lightweight temporal modulation framework within the TRELLIS decoder that enables both texture and geometry to evolve continuously as a function of a learned temporal index.

## 3 Follow-Up Works and Extensions of TRELLIS

TRELLIS has rapidly spawned a family of follow-up work exploring extensions in representation, editability, and dynamic generation. We organize these into three threads.

### 3.1 Advances in 3D Representation [TRELLIS.2, UniLat3D]

TRELLIS.2[11] extends the original framework by introducing *Omni-Voxels (O-Voxels)*, a unified sparse voxel representation that jointly encodes geometry and full physically-based appearance parameters in a single latent structure, compressing objects into approximately 9,600 tokens via a Sparse Compression VAE with 16× spatial downsampling. By coupling geometry and appearance in one representation rather than two sequential stages, TRELLIS.2 achieves higher fidelity on complex topology including thin structures, inner surfaces, and transparency, and serves as the backbone for subsequent 4D generation work. The tighter representation coupling motivates a central question that DynaMesh also confronts: whether temporal dynamics in geometry and texture can be controlled jointly or must be factored separately.

UniLat3D[12] takes a critical view of TRELLIS's two-stage decoupled design, arguing that sequential generation causes systematic geometry-texture misalignment — the appearance stage is conditioned on but cannot correct geometric errors, creating inconsistencies that compound across diverse object categories. UniLat3D proposes a unified geometry-appearance VAE that encodes both into a single latent in one pass, achieving superior coherence. While UniLat3D addresses static generation, its analysis reinforces a central premise of DynaMesh: that temporal modulation of geometry and texture must ultimately be treated as a coupled problem, as the two representations are not independently interpretable once decoded to a mesh surface.

### 3.2 Editing and Controllable Generation [SpaceControl, Steer3D, StyleSculptor, FlowBender]

*SpaceControl*[13] introduces training-free test-time geometry control by encoding user-specified shape proxies — from coarse superquadrics to detailed meshes — into TRELLIS's latent space and blending them with the image-conditioned SLaT via a tunable fidelity parameter. The texture stage then follows naturally from the conditioned geometry, confirming that geometry is the upstream controllable variable in the TRELLIS pipeline. However, SpaceControl produces a single fixed output for each control signal; it offers no mechanism for a generated object to evolve over time. DynaMesh instead treats the temporal index as the control variable, parameterizing a continuous trajectory through shape and appearance space rather than selecting a fixed geometric configuration.

*Steer3D*[14] trains separate ControlNet-style adapters for each of TRELLIS's two stages, enabling independent text-guided modification of geometry or texture without altering the base model. The geometry adapter steers the structural stage; the texture adapter steers appearance — providing direct empirical evidence that the two-stage pipeline supports separate controllable interventions at the stage level. Steer3D produces a single edited static mesh per steering input. DynaMesh extends this stage-level separability into a temporal framework: rather than redirecting generation toward a single target configuration, we learn a time-parameterized modulation of the decoder's output projection that continuously varies both texture and geometry across a sequence of target states.

*StyleSculptor*[15] achieves zero-shot style-controllable 3D generation through a Style Disentangled Attention mechanism that separately extracts geometry-relevant and texture-relevant style features from a reference image, enabling exclusive geometry-only or texture-only stylization of generated assets. StyleSculptor's success confirms separability at the attention level: geometric and appearance representations within the SparseTransformerBlocks carry meaningful independent structure even before the final projection to 101 output channels. While StyleSculptor applies a single reference style to a static output, its disentanglement insight motivates DynaMesh's architectural choice to intervene at the output projection layer — the point at which geometry and color channels are explicitly split — to ensure clean temporal modulation without cross-channel contamination.

*FlowBender*[16] fine-tunes TRELLIS.2's texture transformer using LoRA adapters with feedback-aware training, correcting systematic appearance artifacts in the base model's texture stage while the geometry backbone remains frozen. FlowBender provides direct validation of texture-only LoRA fine-tuning within the TRELLIS family — the closest static antecedent to DynaMesh's color-channel adaptation. Critically, however, FlowBender corrects a single static texture; it does not introduce temporal variation, time-conditioned adaptation, or any mechanism for appearance to change as a function of a temporal index. DynaMesh extends the LoRA paradigm temporally: our out_layer hook learns a time-indexed color modulation supervised by a target video sequence, enabling the object's appearance — and ultimately its geometry — to track a continuous temporal trajectory.

### 3.3 Dynamic and 4D Generation [MORPHOS, SS4D, Helix4D, ArtiLatent, T2Mo]

*MORPHOS*[17] extends TRELLIS to 4D generation by introducing Temporal Structured Latents (T-SLaTs), a unified representation that jointly encodes geometry and appearance along the temporal dimension, and training two autoregressive flow models to generate 4D sequences with topological coherence across frames. MORPHOS directly validates the core motivation of DynaMesh: that frame-wise generation from a static model is insufficient for temporally dynamic objects. However, MORPHOS requires a large-scale autoregressive 4D model trained on diverse dynamic object data. DynaMesh pursues a complementary, parameter-efficient direction: adapting a pretrained static TRELLIS decoder with as few as 576 additional parameters, using a single target video as temporal supervision — a far more accessible setting that does not require retraining the generative backbone.

*SS4D*[18] proposes native 4D generation by lifting TRELLIS-lineage sparse voxel latents into a spacetime representation via factorized 4D convolutions and temporal attention, generating temporally consistent 3D Gaussian sequences from image or video inputs. Like MORPHOS, SS4D requires architectural modifications to the full generative pipeline and access to 4D training data. *Helix4D*[19] similarly builds on TRELLIS.2's O-Voxel backbone, adding sliding-window cross-frame attention to generate temporally coherent 4D meshes with complex topology. In contrast, DynaMesh neither retrains the generative backbone nor requires 4D datasets: temporal variation is injected post hoc into the existing static decoder via a lightweight adapter trained on a single object's video, making the approach practical for single-object dynamic generation without large-scale 4D supervision.

*ArtiLatent*[20] extends the TRELLIS sparse voxel representation to articulated objects by modeling part geometry and articulation dynamics in a unified VAE latent space, with an articulation-aware appearance decoder that conditions texture generation on the current kinematic state. This represents the clearest example in the TRELLIS literature of geometry-state-conditioned texture generation: the appearance of the generated mesh changes as a function of its physical configuration. DynaMesh generalizes this conditioned-appearance idea from discrete articulation states and structured kinematic models to continuous, unstructured temporal deformation learned from video — enabling arbitrary time-varying dynamics without predefined part decompositions or kinematic constraints.

*T2Mo*[21] introduces feed-forward dynamic 3D generation conditioned on both 3D point trajectories and text, separating motion control (where specific points move) from appearance semantics (what it looks like), while generating geometry and texture jointly. T2Mo demonstrates that dynamic 3D generation can be conditioned on explicit spatial guidance. DynaMesh differs in supervision modality and target: rather than 3D trajectories and text, we supervise from a 2D video sequence and a learned temporal index, recovering both appearance and geometry evolution without requiring explicit 3D trajectory annotations or semantic text descriptions.

## 4 3D Morphing and Interpolation

A parallel line of work addresses smooth transitions between 3D objects, providing additional evidence for the feasibility of voxel-space interpolation as a mechanism for dynamic generation. *MorphAny3D*[22] (CVPR 2026) demonstrates training-free 3D morphing between arbitrary object categories by blending SLaT features within TRELLIS's attention mechanism, using Morphing Cross-Attention for structural coherence and Temporal-Fused Self-Attention for frame consistency. The direct interpolation of SLaT features validates our core voxel-space correspondence hypothesis: meaningful transitions in both geometry and texture are achievable by operating in the SLaT domain rather than in mesh space, where correspondence is undefined across different topologies. However, MorphAny3D is a training-free interpolation between two fixed static endpoints; it does not model the temporal evolution of a single object over time, nor does it provide a learned model queryable at arbitrary temporal indices with physically grounded dynamics.

*Interp3D*[23] (ICLR 2026) tackles textured 3D morphing through a three-phase pipeline — condition space interpolation, SLaT structure interpolation for geometry, and texture fusion for appearance — explicitly treating geometry and texture interpolation as separate, sequentially applied operations. Interp3D provides the most direct empirical evidence that geometry (SLaT structure) and color (texture channels) can be modulated in independent phases without corrupting one another, reinforcing the channel-level separability that DynaMesh exploits. Unlike Interp3D, which applies linear interpolation between two static endpoints without temporal supervision, DynaMesh learns a continuous parametric model of temporal evolution from video, enabling it to capture non-linear, physically grounded appearance dynamics — such as the irregular spread of surface degradation — that linear interpolation cannot reproduce. *Wukong's 72 Transformations*[24] (NeurIPS 2025) similarly demonstrates training-free 3D morphing via flow-based SLaT blending with optimal transport, providing further evidence that the SLaT domain supports smooth interpolation across diverse geometry and texture configurations.

## 5 DynaMesh: What We Add

Taken together, the above body of work establishes that (i) TRELLIS's structured latent representation is a powerful substrate for high-fidelity static mesh generation; (ii) geometry and texture channels, while ultimately coupled at the surface, support meaningful independent interventions at the stage and channel level; (iii) voxel-space interpolation in the SLaT domain produces coherent transitions in both geometry and texture; and (iv) generating temporally dynamic 3D objects is an open problem that existing approaches address only through large-scale 4D model training, which requires 4D datasets and full architectural retraining.

DynaMesh addresses the intersection of these threads: we seek to generate meshes whose geometry and texture evolve continuously over time, learned from a single video sequence of the target object, without retraining the TRELLIS generative backbone. Concretely, we introduce a lightweight LoRA adapter that hooks directly onto the TRELLIS decoder's output projection layer — the point at which geometry and color channels are explicitly separated — enabling clean, independently controllable temporal modulation of each channel type with minimal additional parameters. A time-conditioned adapter learns to map a temporal index to per-channel color and geometry deltas, supervised by a differentiable render loss against target video frames. Unlike SpaceControl and Steer3D, DynaMesh targets temporal evolution rather than a single edited static state. Unlike FlowBender and our own prior color-only ablations, DynaMesh modulates both texture and geometry channels jointly. Unlike MORPHOS, SS4D, and Helix4D, DynaMesh requires no 4D training data and does not modify the generative backbone. And unlike MorphAny3D and Interp3D, DynaMesh is a learned temporal model rather than a training-free interpolation, capable of capturing physically grounded, non-linear dynamics from video supervision alone.
